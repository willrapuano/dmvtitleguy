# What Is a Title Settlement Fee?

A **title settlement fee** is a charge for the settlement or closing services performed in a real estate transaction. Depending on the provider and the transaction, the work may include coordinating the parties, preparing or collecting documents, conducting the signing, handling funds, and coordinating recording.

The settlement fee is not the same thing as a title insurance premium. It is also separate from lender charges, government recording fees, transfer taxes, HOA or condominium charges, and other transaction costs that may appear on the same disclosure.

## What the Fee May Cover

The exact scope varies, so request an itemized quote. Common settlement services include:

- coordinating with the buyer, seller, agents, lender, and other transaction participants;
- reviewing the contract and settlement instructions;
- collecting payoff, association, tax, and other closing information;
- preparing or coordinating required settlement documents;
- conducting or coordinating the signing;
- receiving and disbursing funds under the settlement instructions; and
- submitting recordable documents to the appropriate land-records office.

A transaction with entity ownership, an estate or trust, unreleased liens, construction financing, a condominium, or unusual lender requirements may require different work. That is why a single advertised fee does not tell you the total cost or scope of every closing.

## Settlement Fee vs. Title Insurance

The settlement fee pays for services connected with conducting the closing. [Title insurance][LINK:/title-insurance] is insurance coverage governed by the terms, exclusions, exceptions, and conditions of the issued policy.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that title-service fees can include a title search, the lender's title policy, and other services associated with issuing title insurance. The fee for the closing agent is also commonly included among title-service fees. On mortgage disclosures, title services generally appear in Section B or C on page 2 of the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure. An optional owner's title policy generally appears separately in Section H. See the CFPB's [title-service fee explanation](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-are-title-service-fees-en-157/).

## Where to Find the Charge on Mortgage Disclosures

For many mortgage transactions, look at page 2 of the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure:

- **Section B** lists services the borrower cannot shop for.
- **Section C** lists services the borrower can shop for.
- **Section H** may show an optional owner's title insurance policy.

The lender determines how a required service is disclosed based on whether the borrower may shop for it. The CFPB advises borrowers to compare the bottom-line total of title-related charges because an itemized title-company quote may group charges differently from the federal forms.

The lender generally must provide the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing for covered mortgage transactions. Use that review period to compare the final charges with the Loan Estimate and ask about unfamiliar items. Some transaction types use different disclosures, so confirm the applicable documents with the lender.

## Do Fees Differ in Virginia, Maryland, and DC?

Yes. Fees can differ by provider, jurisdiction, property type, lender requirements, transaction complexity, and the exact services included. Ask for a current, transaction-specific quote instead of relying on a generic range.

Maryland does not require every settlement to be conducted by an attorney. The Maryland Insurance Administration says licensed title insurance producers and title insurance producer independent contractors—including, but not limited to, attorneys—may provide escrow, closing, or settlement services. It also notes that non-premium settlement-service fees are not regulated and may differ among title agents. See the MIA's [title producer FAQ](https://insurance.maryland.gov/Producer/pages/titlefaqs.aspx) and [consumer title-insurance guidance](https://insurance.maryland.gov/Consumer/Pages/Title-Insurance.aspx).

## How to Compare Settlement Quotes

Compare the full itemized total, not one headline fee. Ask each provider:

1. What services are included in the settlement or closing fee?
2. Which charges are title insurance premiums, and which are service fees?
3. Are search, examination, document preparation, signing, courier, wire, or recording-coordination charges listed separately?
4. Which fees can change if the property, lender, ownership structure, or closing method changes?
5. Which title insurance policy and endorsements are included in the quote?
6. Who should answer questions about taxes, lender charges, or contract allocations outside the title company's role?

The CFPB recommends using the Loan Estimate to identify services that can be shopped and comparing providers on both cost and service. See its guide to [shopping for title insurance and closing services](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/close/shop-for-title-insurance-and-other-closing-services/).

## Who Pays the Settlement Fee?

Responsibility depends on the contract, lender requirements, jurisdiction, and services performed for each party. Do not assume a customary allocation controls your transaction. Review the executed contract and itemized settlement figures with the appropriate real estate, lender, settlement, tax, or legal professional.

## Frequently Asked Questions

## What is a title settlement fee?

It is a fee for settlement or closing services performed in a real estate transaction. The included work varies by provider and transaction, so request an itemized description.

## Is a settlement fee the same as title insurance?

No. A settlement fee pays for closing-related services. Title insurance is a separate insurance product governed by the issued policy.

## Where does the settlement fee appear on a Loan Estimate?

Title services commonly appear in Section B or C on page 2 of the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure. The section depends on whether the borrower is permitted to shop for the service. An optional owner's title policy generally appears separately in Section H.

## Can I shop for settlement services?

Often, but not always. Review Section C of the Loan Estimate and the provider list from the lender. Ask the lender whether it will work with a provider that is not on its list.

## Does Maryland require an attorney to conduct every closing?

No. Maryland requires the people and businesses performing covered title and settlement work to hold the appropriate title insurance producer licenses. Attorneys may be licensed producers, but the Maryland Insurance Administration's definition is not limited to attorneys.

## How much should a settlement fee cost?

There is no single reliable DMV-wide amount. Request an itemized, transaction-specific quote and compare the total title-related charges, included services, policy premiums, and possible add-ons.
